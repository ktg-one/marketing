# The End of the Software Engineer (As We Know It)

I've been watching the numbers. And they're telling a story most engineers don't want to hear.

---

## THE DATA

At top-tier startups, AI agents now write **40% of production code**.

Not unit tests. Not documentation. Production code that ships to real users.

GitHub Copilot was the opening act. The new wave—Claude Code, Cursor, Kimi CLI, Gemini CLI—doesn't assist. It *replaces*.

---

## THE SHIFT

The bottleneck was never typing speed.

It was three things:
• Understanding requirements (communication)
• Making architectural decisions (judgment)  
• Debugging complex systems (pattern recognition)

AI is rapidly consuming the first and third. The second is next.

---

## THE EMERGENCE

The engineers who survive this transition won't be the best coders.

They'll be the best **AI whisperers**.

Three capabilities separate the orchestrators from the automated:

1. **Precision specification** — describing intent with clarity AI can execute
2. **Critical verification** — knowing when output is confident and wrong
3. **Systems integration** — weaving AI-generated pieces into coherent architecture

---

## THE THREE SKILLS

If you're engineering in 2025, here's where to place your bets:

→ **Systems thinking** — see the whole, not just the function
→ **AI orchestration** — chain agents, manage context, handle failure modes
→ **Domain expertise** — deep knowledge that can't be synthesized from training data

---

## THE 2028 DEADLINE

By 2028, "software engineer" will mean "person who manages AI agents."

Pure coders will be automated away.
Orchestrators will thrive.

The question isn't whether this happens.

It's whether you're positioning yourself on the right side of it.
