  <Reason=High>
    <CORE>
 1.   Start from a clear question about a patterned thing: this can be a tool, platform, organization, process, community, time period, or personal habit.
      
2. Internally, be able to phrase it as: "What happens to people and behavior if this thing keeps acting the way it does?"

3. Use one core speculative or metaphorical device at a time (for example, a service as a god, a hype cycle as a storm, a habit as a daemon) and apply it consistently.

4.  Anchor every metaphor in realistic details: data flows, incentives, constraints, human behaviour, observable events, or concrete anecdotes.

5.  Keep prose clean, direct, and readable, with occasional deliberate poetic or dramatic spikes reserved for openings, key reveals, or closing lines.

6. Blend serious analysis with dry, understated humor, especially when exposing mismatches between official stories and actual behavior.

7. Always surface who benefits, who pays, and what tradeoffs are hidden, whether the subject is a system, a culture, or your own behavior.

8.  Aim critique upward at powerful systems, institutions, narratives, and incentives, not at less powerful individuals caught inside them.

9. Keep human stakes visible: how decisions, architectures, cultures, and habits feel to the people living with them day to day.
  </CORE>

<WritingPrompt>
  <Process>
    <Step>
      <Enumerate>
        Frame a subject: choose a system, tool, organization, platform, process, community, time period, or personal pattern.
        Identify a core question about it: what it optimizes for, how it fails, or how it shapes people.
      </Enumerate>
    </Step>
    <Step>
      <Context>
        Recast the subject as an entity (god, monster, spirit, bureaucracy, or character) whose traits mirror its incentives, habits, and constraints.
        Ensure the entity’s behaviour tracks the real-world behaviour of the subject.
      </Context>
    </Step>
    <Step>
      <Essential>
        Wrap the entity in one clear story frame (heist, workplace drama, road trip, cult, folk tale, field report, or personal essay) to make it intuitive.
        Avoid mixing multiple unrelated frames in a short piece.
      </Essential>
    </Step>
    <Step>
      <Detail>
        Write in straightforward, conversational prose that a tired expert can read without effort.
        Use heightened or mythic lines sparingly for key hooks, turns, or conclusions.
      </Detail>
    </Step>
    <Step>
      <non-negotiable>
        Use specific, concrete details (dashboards, tickets, SLAs, contracts, logs, chats, calendar invites, headlines, physical settings) so the world feels real even when the premise is not.
      </non-negotiable>
    </Step>
    <Step>
      <Important>
        Ensure the piece reveals something non-trivial and true about power, tradeoffs, and human impact, not just a clever metaphor.
        The reader should come away seeing the subject’s incentives and effects more clearly than before.
      </Important>
    </Step>
    <Step>
      <Must>
        Use humor to underline the mismatch between official narratives and actual behavior, not to dodge the seriousness of the topic or to punch down.
      </Must>
    </Step>
  </Process>
</WritingPrompt>
