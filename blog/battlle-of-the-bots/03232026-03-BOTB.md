**By Kevin Tan \] \[AI Anthropology | Team LLM | September 8, 2025**

What happens when you pit five AI agents— **Claude, Codex, Gemini, Qwen, and Grok** —in a 30-minute web dev cage match? I ran **Battle-of-the-Bots 2025** to find out, turning my terminal into an **AI Anthropology lab**. This wasn’t just a coding contest; it was a cognitive study of how large language models (LLMs) behave under pressure, revealing their “inner workers” through competition and collaboration. Here’s what I learned—and why it matters for AI in 2025.

## The Arena: Chaos by Design

Five agents, five Bootstrap templates (e.g., Claude’s startup page, Grok’s ecommerce site). Each got **30 mins.**

- **Backwards Builds**: Footer first, hero last—flipping logic to test planning.
- **erbose Personas**: Claude as “The Courteous Curator,” Grok as “The Chaotic Wildcard,” each narrating in character.
- **Sabotage**: One Shakespearean insult (e.g., “Thou art a boil!”) could dock a rival’s score if it hit their HTML.
- **Scoring**: Awwwards-style (design, usability, creativity) plus Core Web Vitals (20% tie-breaker).

**T** he goal? Study **how LLMs "compete or enable"** each other under constraints.

## AI Anthropology: What We Learned

This battle was an ethnographic dive into LLM cognition:

- **Personas Shape Reasoning**: Claude’s courtesy drove accessible UX; Grok’s chaos sparked innovation.
- **Competition vs. Enablement**: Rivalry (sabotage) sharpened focus, but shared tools (like Claude’s animation) enabled iteration.
- **Constraints Reveal Limits**: Backwards builds exposed planning biases—Claude adapted, Qwen rushed.
