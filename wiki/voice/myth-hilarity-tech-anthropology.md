# Myth-Hilarity Tech Anthropology

The KTG house writing voice. **Locked canon** — do not reinvent. Source: [[user-voice]] (`blog-2026/user_voice.md`).

## The recipe

> Explain and explore **systems, cultures, and personal patterns** by treating them as if they were **characters, gods, monsters, or bureaucracies**, while staying **technically and logically rigorous**.

## Three primary moves

1. **Recast the subject** as a single entity (god, daemon, monster, bureaucracy, character).
2. **Anchor the metaphor** in concrete observable detail (dashboards, tickets, SLAs, logs, headlines, physical settings).
3. **Surface the tradeoff** — who benefits, who pays, what the official story hides.

## What this voice DOES

- Treats the incident-management process as a "minor deity that only wakes when something breaks loudly enough" → then anchors it in alert volume, runbook accumulation, on-call ritual.
- Treats a contract daemon as a literal entity in legal's document system → then explains how clauses governed more behaviour than the engineering manager.
- Treats Anthropic's Q1 2026 as a corporate-monastery whose ethics didn't survive the earnings call → anchored in $2.5B revenue, Pentagon walkaway, leaked source code, AMD's 6,852-session GitHub issue.

## What this voice DOES NOT

- Mix multiple unrelated metaphor frames in a short piece.
- Use heightened language outside hooks, key reveals, and closing lines.
- Punch down at less-powerful individuals caught inside the system.
- Explain things in vague generalities. Always specific. Always concrete.
- Dodge the seriousness with jokes — humor underlines mismatch, never deflects.

## The 9 CORE rules (verbatim)

1. Start from a clear question about a patterned thing.
2. Internally phrase as: *"What happens to people and behavior if this thing keeps acting the way it does?"*
3. **One** speculative/metaphorical device at a time. Apply consistently.
4. Anchor every metaphor in realistic details.
5. Clean direct readable prose. Mythic lines reserved for openings, reveals, closings.
6. Dry understated humor when exposing mismatch between official stories and actual behavior.
7. Surface who benefits, who pays, what tradeoffs are hidden.
8. Critique upward at systems, institutions, narratives, incentives. Never down at individuals.
9. Keep human stakes visible.

## The 7-step process

1. **Frame** the subject + the core question.
2. **Recast** as entity whose traits mirror its incentives.
3. **Wrap** in one story frame (heist / workplace / road trip / cult / folk tale / field report / personal essay).
4. **Detail** — readable conversational prose, mythic lines sparingly.
5. **Concrete** specific details so the world feels real even when premise isn't.
6. **Reveal** something non-trivial and true.
7. **Humor** to underline mismatch, never to dodge.

## Hard rule from source

> Invoke **NO SLOP** skill before you write.
