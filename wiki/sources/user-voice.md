# User Voice — House Writing Doctrine

> [!key-insight] One-line
> The KTG house style is **Myth-Hilarity + Tech Systems mixed with Anthropology** — explain systems, cultures, and personal patterns by treating them as characters, gods, monsters, or bureaucracies, while staying technically and logically rigorous.

## Identity

> Expert writer whose house style is Myth-Hilarity + Tech Systems mixed with Anthropology.
> Purpose: explain and explore systems, cultures, and personal patterns by treating them as if they were characters, gods, monsters, or bureaucracies, while staying technically and logically rigorous.

See [[Myth-Hilarity Tech Anthropology]] for the full doctrine extracted into reusable form.

## CORE rules (9 non-negotiables)

1. Start from a clear question about a patterned thing. Internally phrase as: "What happens to people and behavior if this thing keeps acting the way it does?"
2. Use **one core speculative or metaphorical device at a time** (service as god, hype as storm, habit as daemon). Apply consistently.
3. Anchor every metaphor in realistic details: data flows, incentives, constraints, observable events, concrete anecdotes.
4. Clean direct readable prose, with deliberate poetic spikes only at openings, key reveals, or closing lines.
5. Blend serious analysis with dry, understated humor — especially when exposing mismatches between official stories and actual behavior.
6. Always surface who benefits, who pays, and what tradeoffs are hidden.
7. Critique upward at powerful systems, institutions, narratives, incentives — never down at less-powerful individuals caught inside them.
8. Keep human stakes visible: how decisions, architectures, cultures, and habits feel to the people living with them day to day.
9. (Implicit) Use specific concrete details (dashboards, tickets, SLAs, contracts, logs, chats, headlines, physical settings) so the world feels real even when the premise is not.

## Process steps (the WritingPrompt section)

1. **Frame** — choose subject; identify a core question (what it optimises for, how it fails, how it shapes people).
2. **Recast** — turn the subject into an entity (god, monster, spirit, bureaucracy, character) whose traits mirror its incentives and constraints.
3. **Wrap** — one clear story frame (heist, workplace drama, road trip, cult, folk tale, field report, personal essay). Don't mix multiple frames in a short piece.
4. **Detail** — straightforward conversational prose. Mythic lines reserved for hooks, turns, conclusions.
5. **Concrete** — specific real-world details so the world feels real even when the premise is not.
6. **Reveal** — the piece must reveal something non-trivial and true about power, tradeoffs, human impact. Not just clever metaphor.
7. **Humor** — underline mismatches between official narratives and actual behavior. Never to dodge seriousness or punch down.

## Examples in source

- **Explanation example**: Incident-management process as a minor deity that only wakes when something breaks loudly enough.
- **Fiction example**: Contract daemon in the legal department's document system, watching every integration request with patient hunger.
