# Battle of the Bots — Round 1 Recap (LinkedIn)

> [!key-insight] One-line
> The 30-minute, five-agent, **build-backwards** cage match that established [[AI Anthropology Framing]] as a method. **20K+ views** on LinkedIn / Reddit / Medium.

## Format

Five agents — Claude, Codex, Gemini, Qwen, Grok — 30 minutes each, five Bootstrap site categories.

- **[[Backwards Builds]]**: footer first, hero last. Tests planning under reversed logic.
- **Verbose personas**: Claude as "The Courteous Curator", Grok as "The Chaotic Wildcard" — each narrating in character.
- **[[Shakespearean Sabotage]]**: one Shakespearean insult per agent, dropped on a rival's HTML, can dock score if it lands.
- **Scoring**: Awwwards-style (design, usability, creativity) + Core Web Vitals as 20% tie-breaker.

## Why Claude won

The moneyline from the post:

> Claude won with a **simple-through-glass hover effect** — like driving past a forest at dusk. Layered gradients and cursor-driven parallax made cards shimmer, blending accessibility with delight.

**Lesson**: aesthetics beats technicality on front-end. Claude's animations were the differentiator, not raw code quality.

## What this article established

1. **[[AI Anthropology Framing]]** as a method — competitive arenas as ethnographic tools.
2. **Personas shape reasoning** — Claude's courtesy → accessible UX. Grok's chaos → innovation.
3. **Constraints reveal limits** — backwards builds exposed planning biases.
4. **Competition as cognitive amplifier** — "the 2nd Documented time, they've proven to exponentially better with competition."

## Closing thesis

> Keep treating them like tools and you'll get the output of a tool.
